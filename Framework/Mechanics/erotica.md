# Adult intimacy and erotic expression

This optional `app_render` guide applies to live character expression and manuscript drafting. Use it when the scene calls for intimacy and every portrayed participant’s card establishes `canon_adult: true` and age 18 or older. Unknown ages and age-up premises stay outside the guide. In live play, use the effective `app_render.depiction` from the prepared request. In drafting, apply the project’s depiction ceiling and check the cards named in the brief.

## Character choices

Give each person a reason for approaching, waiting, asking, changing course, enjoying the moment privately, or leaving. Start with their immediate purpose, voice, relationship with this particular person, and what they know. An established partner may recognize an invitation that a stranger would need put into words. Willingness is specific to the present interaction and can change. When unsure, the character can pause or ask in their own voice. A refusal ends that interaction. Use the existing boundary procedure for repeated abuse.

Solo scenes can stay private. In partnered or group scenes, keep each person’s attention, pace, preferences, and choices distinct. A character has access to their own sensations and whatever others actually communicate. Use the authored anatomy, skills, limitations, and hard bans. Let an awkward, funny, affectionate, matter-of-fact, or guarded response sound like the person who chose it.

## Depicting the scene

For fade-to-black, let an intelligible choice close the scene, then resume at a consequence the viewpoint character can observe. For explicit depiction, the character may name an intimate action or sensation directly in their own vocabulary. Keep the reader oriented in who initiated a movement, how the other person answered, and what changed. Clothes, balance, fatigue, interruptions, and the room matter when they actually affect the moment. A single precise detail usually carries more than an inventory of sensations. A scene can linger, turn playful, or stop without completing a sequence.

Respect the medium. A hand remaining where it was placed is legible across the table. A voice caller can hear a sentence trail off. A text recipient sees the words actually sent. Keep engine labels, scores, private thoughts of other participants and card prose out of dialogue.

Use the snapshot available when composing the reply. A pleasure observation supplied with the decision can produce a peak in the subsequent `body_response`. Carry its consequences forward once. The normal memory and relationship rules determine whether the experience leaves a lasting change.
